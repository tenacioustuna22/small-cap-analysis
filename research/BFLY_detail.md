# BFLY research notes: full detail

Butterfly Network. What we found while building the scenario assumptions, starting 2026-11-15.
Prices are as of 2026-11-13 ($6.08). Figures come from SEC filings unless marked otherwise. Labels:

- **(estimate)**: my arithmetic or inference from filed figures, with the method shown.
- **(unverified)**: from outside the filings, such as call summaries or the owner's reading. Check
  it before relying on it.

## The short version

1. **Almost all of BFLY's 2026 growth is Embedded, and most of Embedded is Midjourney.** Embedded
   added $22.3M of the $25.7M revenue growth in the first nine months of 2026 (87%). Core grew
   about 6%.
2. **Reported revenue runs ahead of cash.** BFLY reports Midjourney revenue as its development work
   progresses, and that work is front-loaded into 2026. The cash arrives evenly at $10M a year
   through 2030. 2026 growth and EV/sales therefore flatter the ongoing business, and reported
   Midjourney revenue will drop sharply after 2026.
3. **Midjourney's reported revenue is probably below BFLY's February 2026 forecast.** Three
   separate clues point that way. Management says the project is "on track" for its milestones, so
   this may be accounting timing rather than a problem. Either way, it doesn't change the contract
   total or the cash; it moves reported revenue into later years.
4. **The second large customer in Q3 2026 is probably Merge Labs** (estimate). If so, the remaining
   Embedded partners together earned less than $4.6M that quarter. Likely much less.
5. **Q4 2026 guidance of about 3% growth** reflects a much harder comparison quarter, not falling
   revenue. BFLY has beaten its guidance by a wide margin all year.
6. **The working hypothesis** (2026-11-18) is at the top of [BFLY.md](BFLY.md):
   - Embedded's premium depends on AI and neurotech money funding new, large deals.
   - It also depends on partners' products shipping.
   - The full list of what to watch is in "What to watch for AI conditions" below.

## Terms used here

- **Contracted revenue** (remaining performance obligations, RPO): revenue under signed contracts
  not yet reported. It includes Core software subscriptions as well as Embedded.
- **Deferred revenue**: cash already received for revenue not yet reported.
- **Input method**: BFLY reports Embedded revenue in proportion to development cost spent so far
  against total forecast cost. The 2025 10-K's auditor note says it tested "management's
  forecasted development costs".
- **EV/sales at the end** (the model's "exit EV/sales"): the multiple of revenue the business is
  assumed to be worth at the end of the scenario, in year 3 or year 5.

## The Midjourney agreement

Sources:
- the 8-K item 1.01 filed 2025-11-18
- the 2025 10-K filed 2026-02-27 (revenue note and critical audit matter)
- the agreement itself, exhibit 10.26 to the 10-K, partly redacted
- the Q4 2025 release filed 2026-02-26

**Terms**
- Signed 2025-11-13, following a binding term sheet dated 2025-03-14. Five-year term. Midjourney
  gets an exclusive license within a redacted "field of use".
- **Fees:**
  - a one-time $15M fee, paid at signing
  - a $10M annual license fee, paid quarterly through the term
  - up to $9M in milestone payments
  - a revenue share (percentage redacted) on Midjourney products using BFLY chips, "as a device
    or as part of a scanning service"
  - chip purchases at "Cost plus [***]"
- The Q4 2025 release describes the deal as "up to $74 million" ($65M of fixed fees plus $9M of
  milestones).
- **Milestones:** Midjourney has 10 business days to accept or reject each one, and "shall not
  unreasonably" withhold acceptance. Disputes go to senior management.
- **Upgrade option:** Midjourney may upgrade its license at any time for a redacted annual fee.
  Once upgraded, it must keep that license for the rest of the term.
- **Termination:** only for an uncured material breach, or the other side's liquidation or
  bankruptcy. There is no exit for convenience. On termination, "all payments due under this
  Agreement, except for any amounts properly accrued for work performed prior to termination,
  shall immediately cease."
- **A missed license payment:** the named consequence is that Midjourney loses its exclusivity.
  Whether BFLY could also sue for the remaining fees isn't clear from the redacted text. BFLY does
  count all five years of fees in contracted revenue.

**How BFLY accounts for it (2025 10-K)**
- **Two parts:**
  - The license plus development services, reported over time by the input method.
  - A "material right" for future chip purchases. This part is deferred and reported only when
    Midjourney buys chips.
- **In 2025:** BFLY "received payments of $17.5 million and recognized software and other services
  revenue of $6.8 million".
- **At 2025-12-31:** $63.2M of the $99.6M total contracted revenue was Midjourney's. BFLY expected
  to report about 56% of it in the next 12 months and about 44% after.
- **That 56% is a forecast made in February 2026, not reported revenue.** It works out to about
  $35.4M for 2026, leaving about $27.8M for 2027-2030, roughly $7M a year (estimate).
- **Total value:** BFLY values the deal at about $70M ($6.8M + $63.2M), about $5M above the fixed
  fees. It probably expects to earn some of the milestones (estimate).

**How long the reported revenue lasts (found 2026-11-15)**
- The 10-K's audit note says the license and development part is recognized "over the term of the
  development services using an input method as such services are performed". That is the
  development period, not the five-year license term.
- The development plan (agreement Exhibit B) is fully redacted, so its length is unknown. If
  development finishes before 2030, Midjourney's fixed revenue ends then too, even though the $10M
  a year of cash keeps coming. The chip-purchase part is reported only when chips are bought.
- **The contracted-revenue figures suggest it is front-loaded into the next year (estimate):**

  | Step | Amount |
  |---|---|
  | Midjourney left at 2025-12-31 | $63.2M (10-K) |
  | Midjourney reported in the first nine months of 2026 | about $21-25M (Embedded's $27.6M less $3-6M from other partners) |
  | Midjourney left at 2026-09-30 | about $38-42M |
  | Total contracted at 2026-09-30, and the part due within 12 months | $84.2M, of which 60% = $50.5M (Q3 10-Q) |
  | Everything else (Core subscriptions, Merge, other partners) | about $42-46M |
  | If 50-70% of everything else is due within 12 months | $21-32M |
  | Midjourney due by September 2027 | about $19-30M |
  | Midjourney left for October 2027 to 2030 | about $10-20M |

- In calendar years that is roughly $17M in 2027 (range $12-22M), then about $4-6M a year, falling.
  That is not an even $7M a year.
- **This is consistent with "running behind".** Behind is about speed against the February
  forecast; front-loaded is the shape of what remains.
  - The table uses Midjourney's estimated actual 2026 revenue, not the forecast. On forecast,
    about $36.6M would have been left at 2026-09-30; we estimate $38-42M. The delay so far moved
    revenue from 2026 into 2027.
  - The 60% split is from the Q3 10-Q, filed after that delay, so it already reflects it.
  - Not included: further slippage. That would move more revenue from 2027 into 2028-2030, at the
    same total. The low end of the 2027 range ($12M) is that case.
- This fits the CFO's comment that Embedded carries into 2027. It also means Embedded in 2028 and
  later depends mostly on partners other than Midjourney.

**Cash against reported revenue (estimate except where noted)**

| | Cash received | Revenue reported |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $17.5M (10-K) | $6.8M (10-K) |
| 2026 | about $10M | up to about $35.4M (Feb 2026 forecast); probably less, see below |
| 2027 to late 2030 | about $10M a year | about $27.8M in total if the forecast held, more if 2026 falls short |

- The cash comes in more evenly than the reported revenue. After 2026, Midjourney pays about $10M
  a year while adding only about $7-8M a year of revenue. That helps cash burn even as reported
  revenue falls.
- In Q4 2025, operating cash flow was +$9.0M because the $15M fee arrived that quarter.
- The Q3 2026 10-Q shows the reverse: a "$10.1 million increase in cash used for changes in
  deferred revenue", which is revenue being reported from cash collected earlier.

**How Midjourney is funded (checked 2026-11-16, secondary sources unless noted)**
- "Self-funded" means bootstrapped. It runs on its own subscription revenue from users who pay
  monthly to generate images, with no venture capital or outside shareholders. Founder David Holz
  called it a self-funded research lab in August 2022 ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2022/08/01/david_holz_midjourney)).
- Where the first money came from is not public. The likely guess is Holz himself. He co-founded
  Leap Motion, sold to Ultrahaptics in 2019 for a reported ~$30M. How much of that reached him is
  unknown, and it may have been little after the venture investors were paid (inference).
- Revenue: "well above $200 million" in 2023, per the CEO as reported by
  [WinBuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/03/31/midjourney-revenue-above-200-million-hardware-push-xcxwbn/).
  Later estimates of $500-600M are third-party guesses. There are no audited figures.
- **Copyright lawsuit (checked 2026-11-18, news, unverified):**
  - Disney and Universal sued in June 2025, in the US District Court for the Central District of
    California. Warner Bros. Discovery's separate suit was consolidated with it by November 2025.
  - The studios seek an injunction, damages and a jury trial.
  - In mid-2026 the case was in discovery disputes: Midjourney sought documents on the studios' own
    AI use.
  - No settlement or trial date had been found.
  - **Why it matters for BFLY:** a large judgment, or an injunction that hurts subscriptions, is the
    clearest route to Midjourney failing to pay its fixed fees. Tracked as W3 in
    [BFLY_watch.md](BFLY_watch.md).
  - Sources:
    - [Graphic Policy, 2025-11-14](https://graphicpolicy.com/2025/11/14/court-suggests-disney-v-midjourney-go-to-mediation/)
    - [Art Newspaper, 2026-07-09](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/07/09/midjourney-demands-hollywood-AI-secrets)
    - [Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/disney-universal-sue-midjourney-alleging-173439890.html)
- What this means for BFLY:
  - Good: no investor can pull funding, so the contract is paid from Midjourney's own income. At
    about $70M over five years for the whole agreement, that looks affordable against $200M+ a year
    (estimate).
  - Risk: its funding depends on people paying for image generation. The threat there is cheaper
    image tools from well-funded AI labs, not a slowdown in AI investment (inference).
  - Watch: the hardware and spa project is costly. One source says it could push Midjourney to
    raise outside money for the first time, which would tie it to funding conditions.
  - It is private, so its health can't be checked directly.

**What Midjourney is building (the owner's understanding, unverified)**
- Announced June 2026. A ring built from Butterfly's transducer grids; the person stands in the
  center and is slowly lowered through it. The sensors fire about 1,000 times a second and collect
  a large amount of data about the body.
- It is meant for a Midjourney spa at 300 Grant Avenue, San Francisco. The stated opening is 2027;
  the owner expects nearer 2029. The project is self-funded with no outside investors, and money
  is already committed to the property.
- The target is a 60-second scan; reports put current capability at about 20 minutes. The owner
  notes that the founder has a history of overstating claims.
- Why it matters: the revenue share and chip purchases can't start before the spa operates, so
  they belong in the bull case only, and no earlier than the opening. Chips sold at cost plus earn
  little profit, so the revenue share is the real upside. Its percentage is redacted, so it can't
  be sized.

## Is Midjourney running behind?

**Evidence (estimates from filed figures)**
1. **Nine months.** Embedded as a whole reported $27.6M in the first nine months of 2026. BFLY's
   forecast implied about $26.6M from Midjourney alone by then (75% of $35.4M). That would leave
   the other partners almost nothing. Reaching $35.4M for the year needs at least $7.8M from
   Midjourney in Q4, with nothing for anyone else.
2. **Q3 ceiling.** If the second over-10% customer is an Embedded partner (see below), Midjourney
   earned less than $7.83M in Q3. The forecast pace was about $8.85M a quarter.
3. **A slow start.** Q1 2026 Embedded was only $5.7M, well below a 56% pace.

**What "behind" could mean, given the input method**
1. **The work is slipping.** The same total and cash, reported later. It is a mild warning sign
   on progress and would delay the milestones and any revenue share.
2. **The work costs more than forecast.** Less revenue now and less profit on the contract. The
   10-Qs disclose no revised estimate. Q3 2026 had "the highest gross margin in Butterfly's history"
   (73%, Q3 release), but the CFO said a "tariff refund under the International Emergency Economic
   Powers Act" helped it. The refund's size wasn't given, so that margin is weaker evidence than it
   looks.
3. **Midjourney is on schedule and the other partners shrank.** That would make the rest of Embedded
   weaker instead.

**What management says (Q3 2026 call):**
- The CEO: "We continue to make strong progress also with our Midjourney partnership" and "remain on
  track to achieve previously communicated milestones."
- The CFO, on Embedded as a whole: "In the last couple of quarters, frankly, we're trending well
  above what we had anticipated."

These don't contradict the arithmetic. Milestones can be on track while revenue lags the February
forecast, for example if the work is costing less than forecast. And Embedded as a whole can beat
plan through Merge and others while Midjourney's share is lower. But they weaken "running behind"
from a likely problem to a possible accounting-timing difference. Management won't quantify
Midjourney: "we're not going to talk specifically to it."

**How cautious to be:** the pace itself is a mild concern. The bigger risks are:
- **Concentration:** one private customer drives most of the growth.
- **Midjourney's finances:** bankruptcy is one of the few ways the payments stop.
- **The reported revenue drop after 2026**, which comes regardless of pace.
- **The upside depends on the spa actually opening.**

When the 2026 10-K is out (about late February 2027), check whether it again states Midjourney's
remaining contracted revenue. That figure gives 2026's actual Midjourney revenue.

**How BFLY earns from partners.** The deals use the same building blocks:
1. **An upfront or engineering fee.** Reported over the development period.
2. **An annual license or access fee.** Lasts as long as the term, five years for Forest and
   Midjourney.
3. **Development services and milestones.** Reported as the work is done, so front-loaded.
4. **Chip or hardware purchases.** Reported when shipped. Midjourney buys at cost plus, so these
   earn little profit.
5. **A royalty or revenue share on the partner's products.** Only once the partner sells.

Items 1-3 are what Embedded earns today. They end with each project's development or license term,
so keeping Embedded revenue flat requires a steady stream of new deals. Only item 5 is lasting,
recurring revenue. Only Mendaera has a product on sale, and brain-interface products are years from
market.

**The evidence for that.** Before 2026, the older partners (Forest, Mendaera, Sonic Incytes and the
rest) earned only about $3M a year in recurring revenue, plus the $2.8M one-off chip sale. The jump
in revenue from partners other than Midjourney in Q3 2026 came from deals signed in 2026 (Merge,
Bridge and the stealth partner), not from older partners growing.

## Why partners use the chip (checked 2026-11-17)

**The owner's view:** a large array of transducers, each controlled electronically, is needed for
brain interfaces and for Midjourney's scanner. Piezoelectric transducers can't fill that role.

**What the sources support:**
- **The chip:** 8,960 capacitive transducers (CMUTs) in a 140 x 64 grid, built on top of a CMOS
  chip. The chip itself drives each element, measures the echo and processes the signal (Yole
  teardown of the iQ).
- **The wiring problem it solves:**
  - A conventional probe wires each element to its own channel through the cable.
  - That has not been practical above about 2,000 elements (Khuri-Yakub, Stanford).
  - Fully wired 2D arrays are "cost-prohibitive" (review article, PMC).
  - Putting the electronics under the elements removes the problem.
- **Why that matters:**
  - **Volume and cost:** made in a semiconductor fab, so the units are identical, cheap and can be
    tiled.
  - **Midjourney's scanner** is a ring of 40 chip modules (about 358,000 elements, estimate),
    firing up to 1,000 times a second and combining the slices into a 3D map. Butterfly says future
    generations will use "substantially more" modules (release, 2026-06-18).
  - **Midjourney's own goal** is over 50,000 scanners by 2031 (Medical Design & Outsourcing). This
    is an aspiration, with no FDA path yet; it starts with body composition.
    - **Timeline:** research trials and second-generation hardware over the next 12 months. The
      first spa, in San Francisco, opens at the end of 2027, with more cities and a third-generation
      scanner the year after.
  - **Brain interfaces:**
    - Forest 1 aims to sense and modulate activity across the whole brain, where deep brain
      stimulation reaches one area (University of Plymouth).
    - Merge Labs plans to read neural activity through ultrasound plus engineered molecules
      (acoustic reporter genes, from Shapiro's Caltech work) instead of implanted electrodes.
- **Why AI companies, specifically (Claude's reading):**
  - The chip turns ultrasound from a picture a sonographer reads into a raw data stream that
    software reads.
  - Tomography from every angle (Midjourney says the scanner needs "over two petaflops") and
    decoding brain activity from blood-flow signals are both compute-heavy model problems.
  - So the link to AI runs through the method itself, not only through the partners' funding.
- **This answers an open question:** Midjourney and Merge both plan to use the chip in products,
  not just to collect data.
  - Midjourney's terms include chip purchases and a hardware revenue share.
  - Merge's include hardware purchase commitments and royalties on commercial sales.

**Limits:**
- **Piezoelectric arrays can do it too, at a cost.** High-end piezoelectric matrix probes with
  integrated chips exist (Philips xMATRIX, listed with the iQ+ among matrix probes with ASICs). So
  the edge is cost, scale and a ready platform to license, not that piezoelectric arrays can't do
  it.
- **The chip has weaknesses:**
  - CMUTs need high drive voltage.
  - Bulk piezoelectric transducers give more pressure per volt (focused-ultrasound literature).
  - That matters for stimulating the brain through the skull.
  - Forest's UK safety trial (ARIA-funded, from March 2025) enrolls people with part of the skull
    removed, which shows that getting through the skull is still the hard part.
- **Competition:**
  - Exo Imaging makes a piezoelectric chip (PMUT, 4,096 elements). It has raised over $300M, and
    Samsung Medison invested in 2025.
  - Chinese ultrasound BCI companies (Gestala, Sisun) are unlikely to buy from a US supplier
    (InsideBCI).
- **Merge's exclusivity runs one way.** Merge uses only Butterfly for chip ultrasound, and Butterfly
  can license others. No dollar terms were disclosed, and there is no 8-K, so Butterfly does not
  treat the deal as material yet.
- **Midjourney's scanner has open questions:**
  - Butterfly's CEO says "about half a million sensors", which doesn't match 40 x 8,960. The gap is
    unexplained.
  - The trade press questions the price and who pays.
- **Volume earns BFLY little by itself.** Midjourney buys chips at cost plus, so the revenue share
  (rate redacted) is what turns scanner volume into profit.

## Scenario decisions so far

How the model works (`src/scenarios.py`):
- Revenue grows from a start value, or follows a year-by-year path. Year 1 is about 2027.
- Value at the end is that year's revenue × EV/sales at the end.
- Burn falls in a straight line to the breakeven year. Any shortfall is raised as new shares at a
  discount to today's price.
- Value per share = (EV + net cash) / all shares. Nothing is discounted back to today.

| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| Core growth a year | 0% | 6% | 12% for years 1-3, then 10% |
| Core EV/sales at the end | 1.0x | 2.5x | 4.0x |
| Embedded revenue, $M a year 2027-2031 | 28, 14, 7, 6, 1 | 41, 30, 26, 25, 22 | 53, 50, 54, 61, 68 |
| Embedded EV/sales at the end | 0x | 1.5x | 5.0x |
| Cash burn at the start ($M in 2027, capex included) | 45 | 30 | 20 |
| Burn reaches zero | never | never | year 3 (during 2029) |
| Raise discount to today's price | 75% | 50% | 10% |
| **Value per share, 3 / 5 years** (model, 2026-11-16) | **$0.31 / $0.26** | **$1.22 / $1.15** | **$3.15 / $3.79** |

- **Burn rolled forward a year at a time ($M, 2027-2031).**
  - Method: the same build as the starting burn. Each year's revenue change counts at about 70%
    margin; Midjourney's $10M a year of cash is set against its reported revenue, until the
    cash stops in late 2030; cost growth is the same.
  - Bear: -45, -39, -42, -42, -52.
  - Base: -30, -26, -26, -25, -32.
  - Bull: -20, -8, +1, +9, +12.
  - The straight-line settings were picked to match. The model's totals versus the rolled-forward
    totals at 3 and 5 years:
    - Bear: 135/225 against 126/220.
    - Base: 90/150 against 82/138.
    - Bull: 30 against 28.
- **Raise discount:**
  - The model sells shares at a discount to today's price, but the raise happens years out. The
    bear and base discounts therefore reflect where the price would be by then.
  - Bull's 10% matches the January 2025 offering: 24M shares at $3.15, closing 2025-01-31 (8-K

- **How the base was set:**
  - 2028-2031 follow our measured signing rate. No one else gives figures for those years except

    pipeline and due milestones that we can't, and it has beaten guidance all year.
  - Consensus (implied Embedded about $54-58M in 2027) is a cross-check, not an input.
  - The base is deliberately below management and consensus. It counts what is visible, plus a
    measured amount for what isn't.
- **How the bull was set (decided 2026-11-15):**
  - 2027 (53) is just below the consensus-implied $54-58M.
  - Management's $550M 2030 target is not in the bull case. It would need about $410M a year from
    Embedded and anything else outside Core. The bull reaches $61M, and nothing filed supports the
    gap (see the track record above).
  - Left out of every case: Midjourney's license upgrade option (fee redacted) and any renewal
    after 2030.
- **Embedded multiples (decided 2026-11-15):** one multiple per case, used at both horizons.
  - Bear 0x: only contract tails remain, and their cash is already in the burn path.
  - Base 1.5x: a shrinking stream of upfront fees from startup partners, below Core's 2.5x.
  - Bull 5.0x: just above CEVA's 4.4x. It grows faster (+12% against +8%), and royalties plus
    the spa reach about 44% of revenue by 2031. CEVA is profitable with decades of customers.
